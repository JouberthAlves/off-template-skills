# Styles of burger food trucks

Based on: 12 sites (6 US, 2 UK, 2 Australia, 2 Brazil), opened on 2026-10-26
for the burger example of the off-template repo
(`examples/burger-2026-10/off-template/research.md`). Comes with the vault as
an example of a niche note.

A fixed burger shop has the same menu and ordering, but swaps "where we are
today" for a fixed address and hours. The Brazilian sample is thin: both
sites were delivery-platform menus, not the trucks' own sites.

## Keep: what almost all of them have
- Where the truck is today, on the first screen: stop name, hours, a map link.
- A weekly schedule with dates, easy to scan on a phone; an honest "closed for the season" when it is.
- The menu with real prices, short enough to read at a glance.
- A close photo of one burger: melted cheese, crust, pickles.
- One clear order action ("Order online" or "Call") visible without scrolling.
- Instagram, because that's where trucks post last-minute changes.
- Trust facts in one line: halal, beef source, hours, payment.
- A separate, quiet path for events and catering.

## Typical section order
1. Today's stop + the order action
2. Menu with prices
3. This week's schedule
4. Photos / Instagram
5. Catering
6. Footer with hours and contacts

## Avoid: the look everyone repeats
- A hero with only a logo or a slogan, no food and no location.
- The schedule hidden behind an external link or "see our Facebook".
- A phone number as the only way to order.
- The comic palette: red + yellow + black with Bangers or Anton in caps, and emoji.
- Catering pages that read like a marketplace, with dozens of SEO town links.
- Mixing a fixed shop and a truck without saying which is open today.
- Content that only loads by script: an empty page for search and slow phones.
- Template wording ("Taste Extraordinary Now") instead of the name, the street and the price.

## What separates premium from generic
- Premium is a well-shot burger and a schedule you can read in two seconds.
- Generic is the template with a slogan and a mascot.

## Outliers worth knowing
- Smash Up (Annapolis): today's stop is the first thing on the page, above the logo's tagline. It answers the only question a truck visitor has.
- IronMikes: a plain "closed for the season, back in spring" banner. Honest beats empty.
