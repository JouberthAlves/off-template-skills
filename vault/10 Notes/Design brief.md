# Design brief

Without a chosen aesthetic direction, the agent decides on its own, and its
default is the same for everyone. Clean, and generic. The brief is the only
defense against shipping the same page with swapped colors to the fifth
client in a row.

## The 4 questions

1. **What place or object in the real world represents this brand?**
   The palette comes from here, not from a nice-looking hex.
2. **What should a visitor feel in the first 3 seconds?**
   One feeling. If there are two, nothing has been chosen yet.
3. **Two unexpected influences to combine.**
   The collision is what makes something that didn't exist before.
4. **What must this page never look like?**
   The most useful of the four, and the one nobody asks.

If the person doesn't understand a question, don't rephrase it: answer it with
3 or 4 concrete options they can pick from. Example for question 1, burger
place: "a griddle at 2 a.m.", "a 70s diner counter", "a butcher's paper
wrap", "a neon sign in the rain".

Before the direction of a demo or a spec site, also pin down **who pays for
it**: size of the business, ticket, neighborhood, clientele. The concept has
to fit them.

## Anti-convergence rules

These work across projects, not inside one:

- **No hex from memory.** Color comes from question 1, every time.
- **Don't repeat a style.** Read every note in `20 Projects/` first. Colors and fonts can come back; the whole
  combination (reference + emotion + collision) can't. Controlling only color
  and font, over time, leaves you with only odd colors.
- **The collision has to show.** If the two influences of question 3 aren't
  visible on the page, there was no collision.
- **One wildcard element.** Something that doesn't fit perfectly, and that's
  why it sticks in memory (a spec label in mono, a portrait breaking out of its
  band, a ribbon of color through the title).
- **Name the vibe.** 2 to 4 words. An aesthetic without a name turns generic
  the moment you execute it.
- **Run the anti-patterns** ([[AI design patterns to avoid]]) before closing a direction.

## The equation behind every decision

```
Conversion = Desire - (Effort + Confusion)
```

Raise desire with specifics. Cut effort with brevity. Cut confusion with
clarity. Every design decision serves one of the three; if it doesn't, it's
decoration.

## Where the effort goes

Half on the first screen. It's where the page is won or lost, and the only
part every visitor sees.
