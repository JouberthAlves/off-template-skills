# Research: burger restaurant sites

**How it was done.** The network this example was made on blocked most
restaurant sites (a corporate proxy), and the browser couldn't open external
pages. So the skill fell back as `research.md` says: web search on the niche,
one site read directly (Bleecker, London), and published design breakdowns of
the big chains (Five Guys, In-N-Out). No third-party screenshots are kept in
this repo. On a normal connection, the agent opens 8 to 15 sites and
screenshots each one.

## What was looked at

| Site / source | Notes |
|---|---|
| Bleecker Burger (London) | Header with Locations + Order buttons; hero with poster and "Find my local"; locations grid; news; menu teaser; Instagram grid; newsletter; delivery buttons (Click & Collect, Uber Eats, Deliveroo) in the footer |
| Five Guys (design breakdown) | Black background, huge heavy uppercase headings like storefront lettering, crimson red on buttons, headings and borders |
| In-N-Out (design breakdown) | Brand red everywhere on white, black for structure |
| Crav Burgers (Awwwards SOTD) | Ordering concept carried by immersive imagery and motion |
| The Good Burger (dgrees.studio) | Burger you inspect with the pointer, ingredient by ingredient |
| BURGER, Edinburgh (identity) | Block Gothic woodblock type, black or white ink on craft paper |
| Burgee (concept) | Dark chocolate, amber and cream; condensed type running off the edges; explicitly rejects fast-food red/yellow |
| Burger Business, O'Wings, logo roundups | Black + yellow + brick; halftone product shots; chains on red/yellow, "hipster" places on black/white with handwritten type |
| Webflow / Wix / Weblium roundups (FUD, L'Amerikano, Lord Burger template) | Food video hero, rustic brick backgrounds, ordering built into the page |

## Keep: what visitors expect

- An **Order** button visible on the first screen (pickup and delivery apps).
- The **menu with prices**, readable without opening a PDF.
- **Address, hours and "open now"**.
- **Photos of the actual food**, close and appetizing.
- Social proof that is real (Instagram grid, press), not invented stars.

## Avoid: the niche's sameness

- Black background with red and/or yellow.
- Grunge, brick and chalkboard textures.
- Poster-scale uppercase headings on black, used for everything.
- A dark hero photo of a burger with smoke or flames.
- Script fonts and "since 19xx" badges.
- Emoji as menu icons (fire, chili, bacon).

## Premium vs. generic

Premium sites do less: one strong idea (the inspectable burger, the woodblock
type), restrained color, real photography, ordering one tap away. Generic
ones stack textures, colors and badges to look "fun".

## Outliers

- **The Good Burger:** the burger itself is the interface.
- **BURGER Edinburgh:** paper and one ink, no red at all.
- **Burgee:** a warm palette that refuses red and yellow and still reads "burger".

## How the 3 directions use it

- **A, best of the niche:** the BURGER Edinburgh restraint (paper and one ink)
  applied to the order ticket.
- **B, break the convention:** everyone is dark and red, so B is daylight:
  ticket-paper white, marker black, and the order number as the hero.
- **C, outside reference:** the railway timetable: the menu and pickup times
  as a departures board.
